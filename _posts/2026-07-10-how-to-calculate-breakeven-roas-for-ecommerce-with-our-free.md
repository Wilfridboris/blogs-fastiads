## How to Calculate Breakeven ROAS for Ecommerce

**To calculate your break-even ROAS for ecommerce, you must input your total sales, cost of goods sold, fixed expenses, and variable expenses into a profit calculator. The calculator then automatically determines the minimum Return On Ad Spend required for profitability, accounting for all operational costs including returns.** This process takes guesswork out of your ad strategy, ensuring your lean budget isn't wasted.

### Step 1: Download the Template and Gather Your Data

First, grab our profit calculating template, linked in the description below. Go download it now so we can calculate your profits and break-even ROAS together. Once you have the sheet open, we'll start plugging in numbers. Ideally, you’ll have at least six months of data to determine your average profit per sale. If you're just starting, make educated guesses. Hypothesize: if you sell $10,000 worth of products, what would your supplier costs and other expenses be?

### Step 2: Input Your Sales and Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

Begin by adding your total sales in the first section. Let's assume you've generated $100,000 in sales over the last 12 months. The next box is for your Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)—this is how much your product costs from your supplier. For example, if you have two products, you might have spent $20,000 on inventory for product one and $30,000 for product two. Crucially, the COGS amounts you enter must directly relate to the sales figure above. That $100,000 in sales should correspond to the products whose inventory costs you're listing. In this example, your total COGS would be $50,000. The sheet automatically calculates your gross profit and a gross profit margin of 50%.

### Step 3: Detail Your Fixed and Variable Expenses

Next, enter your expenses. Fixed expenses are costs your store incurs even with zero sales, such as Shopify subscriptions or app fees. I've designed the template so you input the monthly cost and the number of months, and it totals the cost for you. Ensure the number of months matches the sales period; if it took three months to sell $100,000, use three months. Variable expenses, conversely, increase with sales volume. Payment processing fees and shipping costs are prime examples. My team and I account for returns here, too. Customers expect easy returns in 2026 and beyond. If you don't factor in that 5-10% of items returned, your break-even number will be artificially low and risky. Adding it here ensures your break-even ROAS is genuinely safe, not just optimistic. This gives you total expenses, your net profit, and your true net profit margin.

### Step 4: Understand Your Breakeven ROAS and Apply It

With all data entered, the formulas in our sheet automatically calculate your break-even ROAS. For instance, you might find that you need each dollar of ad spend to generate at least $2.26 in sales just to break even. Anything above this, you profit; anything below, you incur a loss. This 2.26 is your target ROAS for Google Ads.

## Applying Your Breakeven ROAS in Google Ads

**You apply your break-even ROAS in Google Ads by monitoring the "Conversion value / cost" column, ensuring your campaigns consistently achieve a ROAS at or above your calculated break-even point.** This provides a clear, data-driven profitability target that you can check in real-time across your entire account, down to individual ads or search queries. Without this benchmark, you risk misinterpreting Google’s reported profitability.

### Tracking Profitability with "Conversion Value / Cost"

This is where the rubber meets the road. Open your Google Ads account. You’ll find this metric under the column called "Conversion value / cost." This single column allows you to view your ROAS across your entire account, giving you instant insight into whether specific campaigns, ad groups, or even individual search queries are profitable. For example, if your break-even ROAS is 2.26, you simply need to see "2.26 or higher" in that column to confirm you're making money. If a campaign is consistently below 2.26, it's losing you money, regardless of what other metrics might suggest. This visibility ensures that when Google reports profitability, your bank account actually agrees.

## Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good ROAS for an ecommerce business?

**A good ROAS for an ecommerce business is anything above your calculated break-even ROAS.** For many, a 3:1 or 4:1 ROAS is considered strong, but your specific costs dictate your true profitability threshold.

Can I use this calculator if I sell digital products instead of physical goods?

**Yes, you can absolutely adapt this calculator for digital products by adjusting the "Cost of Goods Sold" section.** For digital products, COGS might include software licenses, server costs directly tied to usage, or developer fees per sale. Your fixed and variable expenses will still apply directly.

How often should I recalculate my break-even ROAS?

**You should recalculate your break-even ROAS whenever your pricing, supplier costs, or significant operational expenses change.** We recommend a quarterly review at minimum, or monthly if you're in a rapid growth phase with fluctuating costs.
